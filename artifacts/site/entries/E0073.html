Measure. cobres ten whereof are equal to three Paris ells. In Persia, and some parts of the Indies, the guze, whereof there are two kinds; the royal guze, called also the guze monkefer, containing two Paris feet ten inches eleven lines, or four-fifths of the Paris ell; and the shorter guze, called simply guze, only two thirds of the former. At Goa and Ormuz, the measure is the vara, the same with that of the Portuguese, having been introduced by them. In Pegu, and some other parts of the Indies, the cando or candi, equal to the ell of Venice. At Goa, and other parts, they use a larger cando, equal to fifteen Dutch ells; exceeding that of Babel and Balfora by ⅜ per centum, and the vera by 6⅔. In Siam, they use the ken, short of three Paris feet by one inch. The ken contains two foks, the sok two keubs, the keub twelve nious or inches, the niou to be equal to eight grains of rice, i. e. to about nine lines. At Cambodja, they use the hafter; in Japan, the tatam; and the span on some of the coasts of Guinea. Tables of Long Measure. 2. Scripture Measures reduced into English. 3.
