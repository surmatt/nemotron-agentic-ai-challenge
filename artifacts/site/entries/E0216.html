From the peculiar structure of the digestive organs of the ox—which are adapted for disposing of bulky and but moderately nutritious food—this plan of inducing him to eat a considerable quantity of straw promotes his health and comfort, and economises the more costly food. The Rev. Mr Huxtable has pointed out a still cheaper way of accomplishing the same end. He shreds his turnips by a machine, analogous to a bark-mill, and then mixes them with cut straw, so that the cattle must eat both together. His opinion is, that he not only gains the element of bulk in this way, but that, from the straw and turnips being swallowed together, the hurtful chilling effect of the latter is avoided.
