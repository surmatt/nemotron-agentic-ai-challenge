EXECUTRY, in Scots Law, is the moveable estate falling to the executor. Under executry, or movables, is comprehended every thing that moves itself, or can be moved ; such as corns, cattle, furniture, ready money, &c. EXEDRÆ, in antiquity, denoted halls with many feats, where the philosophers, rhetoricians, and men of learning, met for discourse and disputation. The word occurs in ecclesiastical writers as a general name for such buildings as were distant from the main body of the churches, and yet within the limits of the church taken in its largest sense. Among the exedræ the chief was the BAPTISTERY.
