AMIDA, a god worshipped by the Japanese, who has many temples erected to him in the island of Japan, of which the principal is at Jedo. The Japanese have such a confidence in their idol Amida, that they hope to attain eternal felicity by the frequent invocation of his name. One of the figures of this idol is represented at Rome. Amida (anc. geog.), a principal city of Mesopotamia (Liber Notitiae); Ammaea (Ptolemy); situated on a high mountain, on the borders of Afyria, on the Tigris, where it receives the Nymphius.—It was taken from the Romans, in the time of the emperor Constantius, by Saporas king of Persia. The siege is said to have cost him 20,000 men; however, he reduced it to such ruin, that the emperor afterwards wept over it. According to Ammianus Marcellinus, the city was raided; the chief officers were crucified; and the rest, with the soldiers and inhabitants, either put to the sword or carried into captivity, except our historian himself, and two or three more, who, in the dead of the night, escaped through a postern unperceived by the enemy.
